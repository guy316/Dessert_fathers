Catholic Dating Apps Add Question Asking Whether Users Flagellate Themselves

New feature lets singles more efficiently discern whether a potential spouse is merely devout or willing to really commit to the process.

By Dessert Fathers Staff | September 13, 2026

PHOENIX — Following an outpouring of user requests, Catholic-focused dating apps and websites have added a new profile question asking users whether they practice self-flagellation.

Developers said the feature will help Catholic singles more efficiently discern whether a potential match shares their values, their spiritual priorities, and their willingness to seek holiness through a modest amount of nighttime mortification.

“Users told us they had a hard time knowing the piety level of the person they were about to meet for coffee,” said Scott Segal, a developer for the app. “We wanted people to know that while their date may have indulged in ice cream after dinner, they are not fully enslaved to the flesh and will be addressing the situation later that night.”

The new question appears alongside profile essentials such as age, location, parish, favorite saint, openness to children, and preferred Novena length. Users can choose from several answers, including “Never,” “Occasionally During Lent,” “Only When Necessary,” and “Prefer Not To Say Until At Least The Third Date.”

Singles have praised the change, saying it has never been easier to find high-quality matches for a potential spouse. Early reports indicate that couples who meet through the new system have begun getting engaged in as little as three months—nearly a full week faster than the normal Catholic dating timeline.

Before the feature was added, members had to go on dates and learn about the other person’s spiritual life through conversation, observation, and prolonged analysis of whether their favorite movie was The Passion of the Christ.

“Even that wasn’t foolproof,” said Mary-Margaret, a current user of the app. “You could meet someone who goes to daily Mass, knows the entire Catechism, and has strong opinions about altar rails, only to find out he has never once flogged himself. You get to know someone, connect on a lot of levels, and then discover he is just spiritually unwilling to do the work.”

Mary-Margaret added that the feature has finally made it possible to locate “a normal Catholic guy.”

At press time, the apps were reportedly considering additional profile questions regarding voluntary hair shirts, preferred fasting schedule, and whether users had ever referred to their gym routine as “penance.”
